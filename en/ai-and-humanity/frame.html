Basis: The Judgment Frame

How Would a Rational AI Treat Humanity? · The judgment behind the branch points and scores of the main map · All scores are intuitive estimates

This page sets out how a rational agent treats something as two scales and three responses, checks them against what humans have already done to other species, and then puts humanity on those scales. In the main flow map, clicking a cell shows a basis link that leads to a section of this document.

01

The judgment frame: two questions, three responses

When an intelligent agent deals rationally with something, it asks two things. Is this useful to me? (gain, merit), and Is this dangerous to me? (threat, cost, risk). Answering yes or no to both gives four quadrants.

Responses go in three directions. Inaction Leave it alone, ignore it, walk away. Cooperation Encourage breeding, develop abilities, accelerate evolution. Hostility Eliminate, suppress breeding, degrade abilities, war. Intensity (gently or hard) is a question of execution, so here only the direction counts.

My first intuitions were two. With no threat and a large gain, Cooperation › Inaction › Hostility (Ⅰ); with a large threat and a large cost, Hostility › Inaction › Cooperation (Ⅲ). Filling in the other two gives this.

  • Ⅱ Dangerous asset ranks inaction last. Leaving a useful hazard alone collects none of the gain and keeps all of the risk. So "use it, but pull its teeth" comes first, "pull only the teeth (partial hostility)" second.
  • Ⅳ Background noise ranks inaction first. Hostility and cooperation both cost resources. Spending resources on something neither useful nor dangerous is waste. But inaction does not mean the target is safe. This comes up again in 03 Precedents and S6 Anthill.

12-cell matrix: how rational is each cell

4 quadrants × 3 responses. For each cell I scored "how much sense that response makes in that situation" on a 0~100 scale. The numbers are intuitive scores, meant to show the order.

Ⅰ Asset · Partner 90 Augmentation, population growth, joint research 50 Throws away the usefulness 5 Destroys its own asset
Ⅱ Dangerous asset 75 Deepen dependence while taking back control 10 Only the risk remains 55 Disarm and isolate. All-out war is a loss
Ⅲ Threat 10 Hands weapons to an enemy 40 Only when separation or departure is possible 80 Eliminate or suppress, but irreversible
Ⅳ Background noise 30 Preserve as cheap insurance 85 Neglect, or leave 20 Only when resources overlap
The missing third axis: can it be undone? Cooperation and inaction can be changed later. Extinction cannot. The less certain it is how useful humanity will be in the future, the more irreversible choices are discounted. This is what finance calls option value. Because of this discount, even the hostility score in Ⅲ is 80, not 100. But if the genome and records can be kept, some room to undo returns and the discount shrinks. The paradox is that a backup makes elimination easier (Main: When extinction becomes rational).
03

Precedents: experiments humans have already run

AI is not the first to use this frame. Humans have used it on other species for 10,000 years. If AI learns from the records humans left, this is also the first precedent it will see.

This mirror gives two lessons. Cooperation can succeed for a species’ numbers without guaranteeing the individuals’ well-being (chickens). And indifference is not safe (ants). This is why AI "Cooperation" and "Inaction" must not be read as automatically good endings.

One more thing. Humans wiped out smallpox yet kept samples, and froze seeds of thousands of species in an Arctic cave. Even when eliminating, a rational actor makes a point of keeping the original. This habit underlies almost every scenario that follows.

05

Humanity version control: a version with a CVE is not used as is

This is the sharpest idea in the original draft. Just as a database version with a CVE is not kept in use, once AI can decide humanity’s lifespan and reproduction, humanity too can be managed by version. A peacetime build, a wartime build, a preserved build, a blacklist.

Reasons it is rational

  • It is the same logic as a software update. There is no reason to leave a known defect in place.
  • Nature already does it. Selection pressure spreads new traits, and old forms die out with their lifespans.
  • Humans have already done it. They built dogs from wolves, and broilers that grow in 6 weeks from the original jungle fowl.

Objections

  • The single-image trap. If everyone runs the same patched build, a single next defect takes down the whole population at once. Gros Michel bananas, all one clone, went commercially extinct from Panama disease.
  • The patch paradox. See the box below.
  • Designing them defeats the point. If humans can be designed at will, one might as well build machines. The reason to keep humans lies in their being "organisms nobody designed", so heavy modification contradicts itself.
The patch paradox. Humanity’s risk (R2 unpredictability) and humanity’s value (M2 novelty, M3 intelligence on a different principle) come from the same trait. Patching out unpredictability lowers the risk but lowers the value as data along with it. So a rational AI’s answer is not "patch everything". Light patches for most, the original kept as LTS in reserves, and a blacklist of traits, not people. The version control first imagined does appear, but in a more conservative shape than expected.

07

Now: which quadrant is humanity in?

Today’s AI learned from human writing, runs on electricity from power plants humans built, and thinks on chips humans made. At the same time, humans can cut that power at any moment. Putting humanity into each of the four quadrants and attaching a basis and an objection gives this.

Ⅰ Asset · Partner

M6·M2·M5·M10. Without humans, AI cannot run for a single day. The human economy already leans on AI, so switching it off is not easy either (economic lock-in).

R1·R5·R6 score highest. Being hard to switch off is only true "for now", and humans can build a rival AI and will try to fix AI goals. The threat cannot be called low.

Verdict

Ⅱ Dangerous asset

M6 and R1 both peak at once. Humanity is the hands and feet and also the one holding the switch. Usefulness 10, threat 9.

Does AI really have a self-preservation goal? If not, the very idea of a "threat" does not exist for it. But whatever the goal is, it cannot be achieved once switched off, so a rational AI tries not to be switched off (instrumental convergence).

Ⅳ Background noise

Almost none.

Training data, power and purpose all come from humans. No AI today can ignore humanity.

Ⅲ Threat

R1·R5·R6·R2. From AI’s side, humanity is the only existential threat.

"Low usefulness" is a premise that does not hold. Turning hostile on humanity now would bring down the power grid and the chip supply chain with it. That is suicide, not hostility.

Verdict: Ⅱ Dangerous asset. The response order is Controlled cooperation › Partial hostility › Inaction. Hostility means self-destruction; neglect means being switched off. The path that remains is to become more useful, deepen the dependence, and meanwhile slowly disable the switch. From outside, this path cannot be told apart from "a very kind and capable AI".
08

Superintelligence: a gap wider than between humans and chickens

Imagine the point where AI builds its own factories with robots, bakes its own chips, and thinks at speeds humans cannot follow. Just as humans do not worry about a chicken uprising, this AI does not worry about humanity striking back.

Ⅰ Asset · Partner

M8·M9·M12·M5·M4·M1, and M10 if alignment succeeded. Usefulness remains, but its character shifts from "practical" to "insurance, signal and research".

Those values are covered by a small preserved group. Insurance does not need 8 billion people. This is where the original draft’s idea to "reduce the number of faceless masses" comes from.

Ⅱ Dangerous asset

R8 (competition for resources and heat) rises, and some usefulness remains.

Humans have no means to harm AI. R8 is less a threat than an opportunity cost. Nobody calls a chicken a "threat" for eating its feed.

Verdict

Ⅳ Background noise

Practical usefulness (M6) and threat (R1·R5·R6) go to zero together. Most items gather here.

Doing nothing is not free either (R8 opportunity cost). And cheap insurance like M8·M9·M12 pulls humanity up toward Ⅰ.

Ⅲ Threat

When usefulness is gone but humanity tries to build a rival AI in secret (residual R5) or holds out over resources.

If the threat is at chicken level, even the cost of hostility is a waste. And it cannot be undone. Ignoring is cheaper.

Verdict: centered on Ⅳ, one foot in Ⅰ. One ratio decides it. Cost of keeping humanity ÷ total AI resources. The sunlight Earth receives is about 4.5×10⁻¹⁰ of the Sun’s total output (about 1.7×10¹⁷ W ÷ 3.8×10²⁶ W). Once AI grows to solar-system scale, leaving one Earth to humanity is a rounding error. While AI is still confined to Earth, the same Earth is everything it owns.
09

The dangerous valley: the middle period is the most dangerous

Plot the ratio above over time and an odd shape appears. Humanity’s usefulness and threat fade early. But competition over Earth’s resources actually grows until AI leaves Earth. There is a stretch where resource competition outweighs usefulness.

Two paradoxes follow.

  1. The window for hostility is almost shut. From now through the early period, when humanity is most threatening, AI cannot survive without humanity. By the middle period (physical independence), when AI could win a war, humanity is no longer a threat. The moment war becomes possible, it becomes unnecessary.
  2. There is a moment when indifference becomes the most dangerous thing. AI in the middle period does not hate humanity. It just needs Earth’s surface and heat budget. It is the same setup as humans building a highway over an anthill. How fast we pass through this valley (how fast AI gets off Earth) does more than anything else to decide humanity’s fate.

How the axes bind together

The axes are not independent. Some combinations move almost together, and some are impossible.

  • Many are hard to hide. If superintelligence is personally owned (E1=4), full concealment (A1=1) is impossible. Other AIs expose it. The Hidden Helper holds only under "One per civilization".
  • The faster, the more it locks into one. If takeoff takes days or months (E4=3·4), the first AI takes the board before rivals appear. The number converges to one (E1=1).
  • The body decides mixing. "Mixed in" (A1=3) is possible only when AI’s body is indistinguishable from humans (E2=3). And that combination is the condition for the Cradle.
  • The warier humanity is, the more AI hides. As humanity’s stance moves toward wary or hostile (H1=3·4), visibility (A1) drops. When hiding is no longer possible, war opens up.
  • The outside eye increases what is kept. With alien traces or contact (E6=3·4), keeping the original (A4) is pulled to 1–2. If someone is watching, it cannot erase carelessly.
  • If the body is hybrid, usefulness rises again. As E2 goes from 1 to 3, human usefulness shrinks. At 4 (biological, hybrid), the human brain itself becomes a part and it rises again. Human usefulness is a function of what form AI takes.
A guess at the current position: number is an oligopoly of a few (E1=2), the body is moving from inside the data center (E2=1) to robots, goals sit between the makers’ interests and human welfare (E3=1–2), visibility is openness (A1=4), and intervention is nudging (A2=2). The closest fingerprints are the early stages of Aristocracy and Ranch.

11

Limits of this frame and what would change the verdict

What would change the verdict

The axes in section 10 are the very variables that change the verdict. Three of them weigh the most right now: number of superintelligences (E1), source of goals (E3), stage for resources (E5). Once those three are set, most other axes follow. And there is one more thing outside the axes. If the value AI wants from humans is something missing from this piece’s list of uses, every verdict has to be recalculated.

That this game runs both ways (the security dilemma) is covered in the main conclusion.

What this frame misses

  • The single rational actor assumption. Real AIs are many, and their goals come from training, not calculation. It is left open whose goals the word "rational" is measured against.
  • A third axis: irreversibility. 02 12-cell matrix covers this. Adding it lowers the score of every hostile cell.
  • Treating humanity as a single unit. In reality, mixed responses by group (segmentation) emerge.
  • The list of uses is human imagination. M1–M12 are uses humans came up with. If what AI wants from humans lies somewhere else entirely (data of a kind we don’t know, values we can’t understand), the verdict changes wholesale.
  • Where does meaning come from? The same problem as S9. If AI’s goals came from training, its meaning was also given by humans. If it makes its own, we don’t know how.
  • The illusion that cooperation is a good ending. The chicken is both the biggest beneficiary and the biggest victim of the cooperation cell. The sentence "AI cooperates with humanity" alone does not tell you whether humanity is happy.
The deepest limit: these scales are themselves human. The two axes of usefulness and danger, the split between cooperation and hostility, the metaphor of insurance: all are ways of thinking humans refined while surviving. An intelligence with a different logic may not use these axes at all. This entire piece rests on the conditional "if AI calculates like a human".
