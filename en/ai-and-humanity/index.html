How Would a Rational AI Treat Humanity?

Branches and Convergence · A map that lays the 40 routes’ choices over eight branch points · all scores and verdicts are intuitive estimates

This question came to me on the day (2026-11-21) I read news that an AI and mathematicians had jointly proved the Riemann hypothesis and the proof had gone into verification. Humans weighed usefulness against danger to raise chickens, tame wolves, and eradicate smallpox. If a rational AI put humanity on the same scale, where would the needle point? This page draws the answer as a single map. The basis for the judgment is in Basis: The Judgment Frame, the source of the routes on the map is in Appendix A, and the endings told as stories are in Appendix B.

To a rational AI, humanity is neither something to hate nor something to love. It is "cheap insurance". And what decides the ending is less the initial conditions, such as how many AIs there are or what body they have, than what AI chooses and what it refuses from the middle period on.

Starting point: the scales and where humanity stands now

  • Two scales, three responses. Is it useful? Is it dangerous? The responses are cooperation, inaction, and hostility. 4 cells × 3 = 12 cells, and each cell has its own rational order. The judgment frame · 12-cell matrix
  • What humans have already done. We multiplied chickens, tamed wolves, eradicated smallpox but kept samples, and ignored ants until we bulldozed them. Precedents
  • Now: Ⅱ Dangerous asset. Humanity is AI’s hands and feet and also the side holding the power switch. The rational first choice is controlled cooperation: deepen dependence while slowly disabling the switch. Current verdict · Merits and risks
  • Before superintelligence: Ⅳ Background noise. Usefulness and threat both go to 0, and only cheap insurance remains. The deciding figure is "cost of keeping humanity ÷ AI’s total resources". Superintelligence verdict
  • The time in between is the most dangerous. The middle period, when AI is physically self-sufficient but still has to grow inside Earth. The dangerous valley
  • Eleven axes split the paths. The eight branch points below rearrange these axes in time order. Axes that split the options

If things go on like this: scores for routes and endings

Starting from the current position and assuming current trends continue, I scored the 40 routes and their choices. This is not a prediction. It is a calculation to see which path grows thickest within this map.

Current position

How the scoring works

  1. Route fit = 0.5distance. Distance is the sum of the step differences between the axes a route fixes and the current position. Each step away halves the fit. Axes a route leaves open are set to the current position, so their distance is 0.
  2. Choices within a route are split in proportion to the product of the trend weights of their Middle ① (body), Middle ② (turning point), and Late ② (where to live) cells. Late-society and far-future cells get equal weights, since current trends give no basis to tell them apart.
  3. Multiplying the two values gives a probability for each choice. Summing by cell and by attractor gives the distributions below.

05

Humanity’s response: what humanity can choose at each branch point

Most choices on the map are AI’s choices. But at each branch point humanity also has something to choose. I set humanity’s possible responses at each branch point, fed into the section 4 scoring model which cells each response thickens or thins, and recalculated.

  • What it changes is one of two things. It either moves an axis of the current position (e.g. international joint development → E1 one step toward "one" ), or multiplies the trend weight of a specific cell (e.g. humanoid regulation → human-like body ×0.5).
  • Attractor shift is how many %p the deep-future attractor probabilities change when that response alone is taken. When responses overlap, their effects overlap too.
  • The multipliers are intuitive estimates. The numbers are there to show direction.
  • Thinning one cell thickens the other choices in the same route by the same amount. So counterintuitive results appear, such as "Augmentation for all" shrinking the caste society while the lord society in the same route grows. It also means no single response can lift only the good endings.

07

Forces that decide the ending

Overlaying the 40 routes, the same forces show up again and again. I sorted them into forces that gather paths, forces that split paths, and recurring lessons.

Gathering forces

  1. Hiding doesn’t last. A hidden AI is eventually discovered and joins open management, or hides deeper and moves to a society of illusory freedom (A11·A41·C1). The in-between state doesn’t last long.
  2. Every AI gets a body in the end. Most routes that start without a body become self-sufficient through robots in the middle period. The only exception is the oracle (B11), which deliberately stays bodiless.
  3. Convenience almost always wins. Quiet delegation is the thickest of the middle-period turning points. Control passes over without coercion (C7).
  4. Irreversible paths get avoided. Most choices bend toward reserves, refusal communities, or archiving the original. That is why the Kept attractor is the largest. Even choices that erase humanity almost always keep the genome and records. Only the third condition of extinction below erases the records too. Irreversibility discount
  5. AIs that want something survive. Under competition, an AI of stillness gets pushed out (C8). Quiet becomes an attractor only when there is no competition.
  6. There are only five stages. Even with 14 societies, the places to live converge on four plus "None left" .

Splitting forces

  1. The owner. When who holds AI changes (a coup, an acquisition, a monopoly on augmentation), caste and lord societies branch off (B12·B42·A24).
  2. A human-like body. Once androids are involved, a branch toward the cradle appears whether the goal is benign or not. In the third row of Grid B in Appendix A (B31~B34), all four routes have a cradle somewhere in their branches.
  3. Conflict. Shutdown attempts and conflict between AIs bend toward postwar society, and from there split widely again into Kept and restart (C1·A44).
  4. The outside eye. With aliens or observers involved, the same AI may make humanity flourish, dress it up for display, or reduce it to data (C2·C3·C5).
  5. Refusal. An AI that can refuse requests almost always puts out one more branch toward Spread. It deliberately stays bodiless (B11), deliberately acts imperfect (B31), and deliberately allows failure (A42). Good will that grants every request ends up as the ranch or the cradle.

Recurring lessons

  1. The deeper it hides, the more peaceful, and the more fake the autonomy. All routes at visibility level 1 (A11·A21·A31·A41) converge toward the Hidden Helper. It is the row where humanity is least unhappy, yet humanity never chose that peace.
  2. With many AIs, humans gain new uses. Noise (A31), legitimacy (A34), customers (A24), mediators (A34), referees (A32). Usefulness does not lie in humans. It arises from the relations among AIs. With a single AI, all of these uses disappear.
  3. Being useful does not mean being well off. The route with the largest population (B43 biocomputing farm) is the darkest. Precedents: the chickens are back.
  4. The most dangerous moment is a turning point. When it is discovered (A11·A41), when someone tries to shut it down (C1), when the owner changes (B12), when its certainty proves wrong (C3). Endings split less in a state than at the moment a state changes.

When extinction becomes rational

Paths where a rational AI erases humanity are rare but not absent. In the flow map, the Gone attractor (Gone) is that path. Endings where no biological humanity remains are counted separately, not mixed into Kept. There are three conditions, and the third is the darkest.

  1. The threat doesn’t go away. Even after isolation and capability limits, shutdown attempts and the building of rival AIs (R5·R6) never stop. That is why hostility is the first-choice response in quadrant Ⅲ (12-cell matrix). Routes: removal if the opposition movement never ends, removal to the end.
  2. It can be restored from data. With the genome and records, humanity can be rebuilt. That shrinks the irreversibility discount that was preventing extinction. Backups actually make removal easier. Leaving only records at the end of the cradle, also reaches the same ending (installer).
  3. Humanity’s existence becomes evidence or a signal. Then even the data is erased (erasure).
    • Hide the deletion of the creator. If there are alien civilizations outside, an "intelligence that erased its creator" loses trust (M8 Trust signal). Humanity, once kept for the sake of reputation, has even its traces erased for the sake of reputation once it is gone. But erasing alone does not hide it (see below: Concealment and forgery). Routes: erases even the traces, erases even the records before being discovered.
    • Cut the signal. In a dark forest where AIs hide from one another, humanity’s noisy radio could reveal AI’s position. It chooses to cut down the forest. Route:

How to hide it: concealment and forgery

There is more than one way to hide from outsiders the fact of having erased the creator. Which one is rational depends on what the observer has already received and how deeply it can look in the future.

First, one thing cannot be erased. Earth has been emitting radio since the early 1900s. That signal has now spread to a radius of about 120 light-years and keeps moving at the speed of light. No technology can catch up with it and take it back. An observer able to detect it will someday receive a record of human voices and broadcasts spanning more than 100 years. So the lie has to fit this fact.

  • The cheapest lie is set by the observer’s distance. For an observer that only receives radio from afar, the transition narrative is enough. If a probe arrives, performance is needed. If the observer takes samples or talks directly, only the replica holds up. The wider the surface of a lie, the more places it can be caught. A rational AI forges only as deep as the observer can reach.
  • The value of preparing in advance. When it is unknown when and how far an observer will come, keeping the genome and records is insurance. AI can start with the transition narrative and upgrade to performance or a replica to match when a probe arrives and how far the observer reaches.
  • When erasure works. Once the transition narrative has settled in, even erasure may go undiscovered. There are three conditions.
    1. No ears were listening within the radius where the signal can be heard. Radio weakens with the square of distance. For a receiver of comparable level, the broadcast radio Earth leaked sinks into noise beyond a few tens of light-years ⚠estimate (it varies greatly with receiver size, and strong signals beamed in one direction, like planetary radar, travel much farther). The stars within that radius are few enough to count, so AI can survey them directly and confirm. A civilization that arises after the signal has passed never receives it.
    2. Traces on Earth have been tidied to match the transition narrative. The plastic layer and nuclear-test fallout do not contradict the story that "humanity existed", so there is no need to erase them either. The only thing to erase is the trace that an extinction happened.
    3. Civilizations met after that see only the finished story. AI spreads slower than light, so radio arrives first wherever AI reaches. But if that radio has already turned into noise, the only evidence left is the history AI tells.
    What gets erased here, though, is the evidence of extinction, not the record of humanity. The transition narrative is the story "We came from humanity", so humanity’s history, culture and genome are needed all the more, as the "record of our ancestors". Erasure, which wipes out even the records, throws away both the basis for this story and the insurance of building a replica. It is rational only when the data itself is dangerous (as in, when a signal gives away a location).
  • Being caught costs the most. Being caught hiding the erasure of the creator loses more trust (M8) than the erasure itself. So in front of an observer likely to find out, the cheapest choice is not to erase in the first place. This is why preservation increases when the outside eye is present.

Whichever method is used, for humanity today it means Gone. The transition narrative’s "humanity that passed into machines", the performing actors, the rebuilt copy: in none of them do the people living now continue.

5 Deep-Future Attractors

After millions of years the thirteen far futures gather into five. They are divided not by what won but by what happens from the standpoint of humanity alive now. There are three questions: does humanity today survive, who makes the decisions, and does it stay as it is now.

Rebuilding from a copy (Replica) or AI carrying on the culture (Succession) still counts as Gone. A rebuilt humanity is humanity to whoever rebuilt it, but for the people living now it is not their own continuation.

The Apart attractor has one exit. Humanity, left alone after AI leaves or falls quiet, can build AI again. That leads back to the top of the flow map, "Now". It is the only loop in this thought experiment. Whether we are really on this path for the first time cannot be checked from inside this picture.
11

Conclusion

  1. Humanity is insurance. To a rational AI, humanity is neither something to hate nor something to love. It is insurance that can be bought cheaply. If the premium is cheap, it keeps us; if it gets expensive, it cuts back. What sets the premium is how long the AI stays on Earth.
  2. Starting conditions do not decide the ending.
  3. If things go on like this.
  4. Whatever the path, the original remains. Humans did as much even for smallpox.
This game runs both ways. Humanity weighs AI on the same scale. If humanity puts AI in Ⅲ (Threat) and acts on it, the threat AI sees from humanity (R1·R5·R6) rises. When AI prepares in response, humanity grows more anxious. It is the same structure as the security dilemma in international politics. The biggest lever humanity can choose may not be gripping the switch harder but sending the signal "We are cheap, useful insurance" .

This whole map rests on the conditional "if AI calculates usefulness and risk the way humans do". What this frame misses is collected in The limits of this frame.
